Anshul A. Nayak

Robotics Researcher

EIB 118, S. Palmetto Boulevard

Clemson, SC 12345

I am a robotics and machine learning researcher developing safe, uncertainty-aware, and human-aligned autonomous systems. My work combines reinforcement learning, probabilistic prediction, Bayesian modeling, and model-based control to help robots operate reliably around people and in complex environments.

My current research interests include uncertainty-aware trajectory prediction, safe robot planning, multi-agent reinforcement learning, active inference, human trust and surprise in human–robot interaction, and vision-language models for robot reasoning.

selected publications

  1. arXiv
    Belief-Aware VLM Model for Human-like Reasoning
    Anshul Nayak, Shahil Shaik, and Yue Wang
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2604.09686, Apr 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML PDF

    This work introduces a belief-aware vision-language model for intent inference in partially observable, long-horizon tasks. Retrieval-based multimodal memory approximates belief, while reinforcement learning over the model’s latent representation refines its decisions. Evaluation on video question-answering datasets demonstrates improvements over zero-shot baselines.

  2. arXiv
    MA-VLCM: A Vision Language Critic Model for Value Estimation of Policies in Multi-Agent Team Settings
    Shahil Shaik, Aditya Parameshwaran, Anshul Nayak, and 2 more authors
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2603.15418, Mar 2026
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML PDF

    MA-VLCM replaces a learned centralized critic in multi-agent reinforcement learning with a pretrained vision-language model conditioned on task descriptions, visual trajectories, and structured team state. The approach improves sample efficiency and produces compact execution policies while supporting return estimation across in-distribution and out-of-distribution multi-agent scenarios.

  3. arXiv
    Prediction Uncertainty-Aware Planning Using Deep Ensembles and Trajectory Optimisation
    Anshul Nayak and Azim Eskandarian
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2502.10585, Feb 2025
    Abs arXiv Bib HTML PDF

    This paper integrates probabilistic human-trajectory forecasts from deep ensembles into robot motion planning. Predictive uncertainty is represented directly in planning constraints, with multiple constraint-satisfaction approaches evaluated on real pedestrian datasets and out-of-distribution corridor-navigation scenarios.

  4. IEEE T-IV
    Cooperative Probabilistic Trajectory Prediction Under Occlusion
    Anshul Nayak and Azim Eskandarian
    IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Vehicles, Oct 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    An end-to-end cooperative perception framework recovers an occluded pedestrian’s current state in the ego agent’s reference frame and forecasts the pedestrian’s trajectory with uncertainty estimates. The method uses relative pose estimation between connected agents and supports uncertainty-aware navigation when direct sensing is obstructed or communication bandwidth is limited.

  5. IEEE T-ITS
    Pedestrian Trajectory Forecasting Using Deep Ensembles Under Sensing Uncertainty
    Anshul Nayak, Azim Eskandarian, Zachary Doerzaph, and 1 more author
    IEEE Transactions on Intelligent Transportation Systems, Sep 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This work presents an encoder-decoder deep ensemble that jointly captures upstream sensing uncertainty and predictive uncertainty in pedestrian trajectory forecasting. Comparisons with approximate Bayesian inference methods show that accounting for noisy perception improves estimation accuracy and produces more robust uncertainty-bounded forecasts, including for out-of-distribution trajectories.

  6. IEEE ITS Mag.
    X-CAR: An Experimental Vehicle Platform for Connected Autonomy Research
    Goodarz Mehr, Prasenjit Ghorai, Ce Zhang, and 4 more authors
    IEEE Intelligent Transportation Systems Magazine, Mar 2023
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    X-CAR is a full-scale experimental connected-autonomy platform built around affordable, high-quality hardware and the U.S. Department of Transportation’s CARMA software. Its sensing, communication, actuation, computation, mapping, and routing systems are designed to broaden access to connected driving automation research and simplify CARMA-based development.

  7. IEEE OJ-ITS
    Uncertainty Estimation of Pedestrian Future Trajectory Using Bayesian Approximation
    Anshul Nayak, Azim Eskandarian, and Zachary Doerzaph
    IEEE Open Journal of Intelligent Transportation Systems, 2022
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This study applies Monte Carlo dropout during inference to standard neural-network forecasting architectures to estimate uncertainty in pedestrian future trajectories. Across multiple datasets, the probabilistic models improve displacement errors relative to deterministic baselines and reveal how dropout rate and forecast horizon affect predictive uncertainty.

  8. SAE IJ-CAV
    Motion Planning of Autonomous Vehicles Under Dynamic Traffic Environment in Intersections Using Probabilistic Rapidly Exploring Random Tree
    Xihui Wu, Anshul Nayak, and Azim Eskandarian
    SAE International Journal of Connected and Automated Vehicles, Oct 2021
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    A Gaussian-process model predicts another vehicle’s future position at an unsignalized intersection and incorporates that prediction into the sampling and steering stages of rapidly exploring random-tree planning. Compared with standard RRT, the resulting probabilistic planner improves safe-path success rates and reduces execution time in simulated four-way-intersection scenarios.

  9. ASME IMECE
    Relative Pose Estimation for Cooperative Vehicles and Tracking of Multiple Dynamic Objects
    Prasenjit Ghorai, Azim Eskandarian, Anshul Nayak, and 1 more author
    In Proceedings of the ASME 2021 International Mechanical Engineering Congress and Exposition, Volume 7B: Dynamics, Vibration, and Control, Nov 2021
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    This paper develops an indirect relative-pose estimation method for cooperative automated vehicles using shared inertial-navigation positioning data. The method expands each vehicle’s effective field of view and combines cooperative perception with detection and tracking of pedestrians, bicyclists, and other vehicles in dynamic or partially occluded scenes.

  10. J. Manuf. Process.
    Modelling, Simulation and Experimental Investigation for Generating ‘I’ Shaped Contour on Inconel 825 Using Electro Chemical Machining
    A. A. Nayak, S. Gangopadhyay, and D. K. Sahoo
    Journal of Manufacturing Processes, Aug 2016
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    The study models electrolyte flow, pressure, turbulence, and temperature during electrochemical machining of an ‘I’ shaped contour in Inconel 825. Computational-fluid-dynamics results are compared with experiments measuring material removal rate, surface roughness, and overcut, followed by grey relational optimization of electrolyte concentration, tool feed rate, and voltage.
